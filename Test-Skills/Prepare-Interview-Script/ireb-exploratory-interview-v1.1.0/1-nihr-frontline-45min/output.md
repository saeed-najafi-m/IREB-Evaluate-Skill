# Interview Script: Loneliness support in English local-authority, private and voluntary-sector social care services

This script is for one frontline staff member ([Interviewee name], [Employer]) from the group of staff who provide or manage support services with a remit to tackle loneliness, in a 45-minute remote interview (video or telephone).

Buffer: 5 min. If time runs short, skip first: Q 9, Q 14, Q 7 optional follow-ups, Q 11 optional follow-ups, Q 4 optional follow-ups, Q 13 optional follow-ups

## Objective

Understand how this frontline staff member sees the way service users experience loneliness, how they deliver support in their daily work, what gets in the way of providing the best possible support, and what they see as priorities for improving services, including concrete good care moments and points where services could improve ("touch points").

## Completion Criteria

- Consent to take part and to audio recording has been confirmed and is on the recording.
- The interviewee's role, typical service users, and the other roles and parties involved in loneliness support are described.
- At least one concrete example of how a service user experiences loneliness has been described in the interviewee's own words.
- One critical task (supporting a person from first contact onwards) has been walked through step by step.
- At least two barriers to providing the best possible support have been named, each with its consequences for service users or staff.
- At least one good care moment and at least one point where services could improve have been described with a concrete example.
- The interviewee's top improvement priorities have been named, each linked to the problem it would solve.

## Welcome and Consent (Opening, 4 min)

> Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. My name is [Interviewer name], and I am part of a university-led research project on how local authorities can better collect and use people's experiences of social care to improve services. Our focus is loneliness. We are speaking with about 20 staff from different regions and backgrounds, as well as with people who use services. There are no right or wrong answers - we are interested in your own experience and views. The interview will take about 45 minutes. Short extracts from interviews may later be used in a film that supports workshops where staff and service users agree improvement priorities; [explain how extracts, recordings and personal data are handled, as set out in the project's information sheet and consent form].

_Have the information sheet and consent form at hand. Do not promise anonymity or confidentiality beyond what the project's consent documents state. Check that the recording is running before Q 1._

- **Q 1** (Required): Have you read the information sheet, and do you agree to take part and to this interview being audio recorded?
- **Q 2** (Required): Before we start, do you have any questions about the project or the interview?

## Role and Work Context (Main, 6 min)

_Capture the setting (local authority, private or voluntary sector) and region without asking for the employer's name if the interviewee prefers not to give it._

- **Q 3** (Required): Could you describe your role and what a typical week of supporting people looks like for you?
  - What kinds of people do you mainly support?
  - Optional: How did you come to work in this role?
- **Q 4** (Required): Who else is involved, inside or outside your organisation, in supporting the people you work with around loneliness?
  - How do you work with them in practice?
  - Optional: Who else would you expect to be involved but isn't?

## Understanding Loneliness (Main, 8 min)

_Possible themes to listen for, not to suggest: life transitions such as bereavement or moving into care, mobility, transport, sensory loss, digital exclusion, stigma about admitting loneliness. Note any moments that could become touch points._

- **Q 5** (Required): From your experience, how do the people you support experience loneliness?
  - Could you tell me about a particular person (without naming them) whose situation illustrates this?
  - Optional: How does this differ between the people you support?
- **Q 6** (Required): How do you recognise that someone you support is lonely?
  - What do people tell you, and what do you notice yourself?
- **Q 7** (Required): What does good support for someone experiencing loneliness look like to you?
  - Could you describe a moment when the support really made a difference for someone?
  - Optional: What made that moment possible?

## Walkthrough of Supporting a Person (Main, 7 min)

_Use the example from Q 5 or Q 7 if one was given. Ask for each step: who is involved, what information is used, and what tools or resources are needed. Note where steps are delayed, repeated or dropped._

- **Q 8** (Required): Thinking of a recent person you supported around loneliness, could you take me step by step through what happened, from the first contact until now?
  - What happened next at each step, and who was involved?
  - Where in this process did things go well, and where did they not?
  - Optional: What information or resources did you rely on at each step?
- **Q 9** (Optional): How do you find out whether the support you provided helped the person?
  - Optional: What happens to that information afterwards?

## Barriers and Their Consequences (Main, 7 min)

_Possible barriers to listen for, not to suggest: time and caseload, funding and commissioning, eligibility criteria, availability of local groups or transport, staff turnover, information sharing between organisations. Ask for the consequences of every barrier named._

- **Q 10** (Required): What gets in the way of you providing the best possible support for people who are lonely?
  - Could you give me an example of when this happened?
  - What were the consequences for the person you were supporting?
- **Q 11** (Required): What consequences do these difficulties have for you and your colleagues?
  - How do you currently deal with them?
  - Optional: Which of these difficulties do you think are specific to your organisation or area?
- **Q 12** (Required): How are the experiences of the people you support currently collected and used in your service?
  - What happens as a result, if anything?
  - Optional: What gets lost along the way?

## Improvement Priorities (Main, 5 min)

_Keep the focus on problems and priorities, not on specific solutions. If the interviewee names a solution, ask which problem it would solve._

- **Q 13** (Required): If you could change one thing about how loneliness support works, what would it be?
  - Which problem would this solve?
  - Optional: Who would need to be involved to make that change happen?
- **Q 14** (Optional): What do you think the people you support would say is most important to improve?
  - Optional: How does that compare with your own view?
- **Q 15** (Required): Is there anything we have not talked about that you think is important for improving loneliness support?
  - Why is this important to you?

## Thanks and Next Steps (Closing, 3 min)

> Thank you very much for sharing your experience and views - this is really valuable for the project. As a next step, the interviews will be analysed to identify good care moments and points where services could improve. [Describe the next steps as set out in the project plan, e.g. the short film of interview extracts, the co-design workshops and whether the interviewee will be invited, and how they can withdraw or contact the team.] If anything else comes to mind after today, you can contact me at [contact details]. Thank you again.

_Stop the recording after the thanks. Note any concrete touch points, examples, and the setting (sector and region) immediately after the interview._

## Assumptions and Open Points

- The domain is adult social care, service improvement, as given in the brief; the interview language is English, the language of the request.
- The guide is tailored to a frontline staff member who provides support; the employer, sector (local authority, private or voluntary) and region are unknown and are captured during Q 3, so the guide also serves the other ~20 staff interviews with minor adjustments for managers.
- Time allocation: Opening 4 min, Main 33 min, Closing 3 min, buffer 5 min (about 10 percent of 45 min, rounded up); total 45 min. No break is planned, as the interview is under 90 minutes.
- The critical task chosen for the walkthrough is supporting one person from first contact onwards, because it exposes most touch points; this was decided without explicit guidance in the brief.
- Q 12 (how service users' experiences are currently collected and used) was added because the project aims to improve exactly this; it may be lower priority if the interviewee has no involvement in it.
- How recordings, extracts and personal data are handled, and whether anonymity applies, is not stated in the brief; the spoken text uses placeholders and no commitments are made. The use of interview extracts in a film refers to service users and staff in the project description; whether staff audio extracts may be used must be confirmed against the consent form.
- Whether the interviewee will be invited to the co-design workshops is unknown and left as a placeholder in the closing text.
- Telephone interviews lack visual cues; the interviewer may need more verbal prompts, which the buffer is meant to absorb.
- Questions about specific people must avoid identifying details; the interviewer should remind the interviewee of this if needed.
