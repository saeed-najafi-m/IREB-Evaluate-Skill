# Interview Script: Loneliness support provided by English local-authority, private and voluntary-sector social care services

Exploratory interview with one frontline staff member ([Interviewee name], [Employer]) from the stakeholder group of staff who provide or manage support services with a remit to tackle loneliness; total duration 45 minutes, remote (video or telephone), audio recorded with consent.

## Objective

Understand how this frontline staff member sees the way service users experience loneliness, how they deliver loneliness support in their daily work, what gets in the way of providing the best possible support, and what they see as priorities for improving services. The results feed the identification of touch points (good care moments and points where services could improve) for the co-design workshops.

## Completion Criteria

- Consent to take part and to audio recording has been confirmed and recorded at the start.
- The interviewee's role, main tasks and goals in supporting people who experience loneliness are described.
- The other roles, services and parties involved in loneliness support (inside and outside the interviewee's organisation) are named, with how the interviewee works with them.
- The interviewee's understanding of how service users experience loneliness is described, including how they recognise it.
- At least one concrete support case has been walked through step by step, from first contact to ongoing support or ending.
- At least one good care moment and at least one point where the service could improve are identified in that walkthrough.
- At least two barriers to providing the best possible support are named, each with its consequences for service users or staff.
- The resources, information and tools the interviewee relies on, and gaps in them, are described.
- The interviewee's priorities for improving services are named, each linked to the problem it would address.
- The interviewee has had the chance to add anything not covered, and the next steps have been explained.

## Welcome and Consent (Opening, 4 min)

> Hello, and thank you very much for making time to talk with me today. My name is [Interviewer name], and I am part of a university-led research project. Local authorities need better ways to collect and use people's experiences of social care to improve services, and our project focuses on loneliness. We are talking to about twenty staff from different regions and backgrounds, as well as to people who use services. Later, what we learn will be used in workshops where staff and service users together agree priorities for improving support.
>
> Today I would like to hear about your work, the people you support, what helps and what gets in the way. There are no right or wrong answers – your experience is what matters. The interview will take about 45 minutes. With your agreement, I will audio record it so I can concentrate on listening; the recording will be handled as described in the information sheet you received. Please avoid using the names of the people you support or other details that could identify them. You can skip any question, take a break, or stop at any time without giving a reason.

*Check that the audio connection is good and the recorder is running before asking Q 1; ask Q 1 again once recording has started so that consent is captured on the recording. Have the participant information sheet and consent form reference [Consent form ID] at hand.*

- **Q 1** (Required): Are you happy to take part in this interview and for it to be audio recorded?
  - If the interviewee does not consent to recording: Would you still like to go ahead with me taking written notes instead?
- **Q 2** (Optional): Is there anything you would like to ask me before we start?

## Role and Work Context (Main, 6 min)

*Keep this section brief; the aim is to understand the setting so that later questions can be tailored. Note the sector (local authority, private or voluntary) if it becomes clear.*

- **Q 3** (Required): Could you tell me about your role and what your work involves in supporting people who experience loneliness?
  - What does a typical week look like for you?
  - What are you trying to achieve for the people you support?
  - How do people usually come to your service?
- **Q 4** (Required): Who else is involved in supporting the people you work with?
  - How do you work together with them?
  - Which other services or organisations do you refer people to or receive referrals from?
  - If family, friends or neighbours are mentioned: What part do they play in the support?

## Understanding Loneliness (Main, 8 min)

- **Q 5** (Required): From your experience, how do the people you support experience loneliness?
  - Could you describe what it looks like in someone's day-to-day life, without naming them?
  - What do people themselves say about it?
  - How does it affect other parts of their lives?
- **Q 6** (Required): How do you recognise that someone you support is lonely?
  - What makes it difficult to spot?
  - What do people find hard about talking about loneliness?
- **Q 7** (Optional): How do experiences of loneliness differ between the people you support?
  - What makes a difference to how lonely someone feels?

## Walkthrough of a Support Case (Main, 9 min)

*Ask the interviewee to choose one recent, typical case. Remind them to leave out names and identifying details. Listen for touch points: moments that went well and points where things could have gone better. Use the follow-ups at the steps the interviewee describes.*

- **Q 8** (Required): Thinking of one person you have supported recently with loneliness, could you take me step by step through what happened, from when they first came into contact with your service to now?
  - What happened next?
  - Who else was involved at that step?
  - What information did you have, or need, at that point?
  - At which moment did things go particularly well for that person?
  - At which moment could things have gone better?
  - If the support has ended: How did the support end, and what happened for the person afterwards?
- **Q 9** (Required): How typical is this case of the people you support?
  - In what ways do other cases go differently?

## Barriers and Resources (Main, 9 min)

- **Q 10** (Required): What gets in the way of you providing the best possible support for people who are lonely?
  - Could you give me an example of when that happened?
  - What were the consequences for the person you were supporting?
  - What were the consequences for you or your team?
  - How do you currently work around it?
- **Q 11** (Required): What resources, information or tools do you rely on to do this work?
  - What is missing or hard to get?
  - What support do you get in your own role, for example training or supervision?
- **Q 12** (Optional): How does your service find out what the people you support think about the support they receive?
  - What happens with that feedback?
  - What makes it hard to collect or use?

## Improvement Priorities (Main, 7 min)

- **Q 13** (Required): What do you see as the most important things to improve in the support for people who are lonely?
  - Which problem would that address?
  - Who would benefit most from it?
  - What would need to happen for that change to be possible?
- **Q 14** (Required): If you could design loneliness support from scratch, what would it look like for the people you support?
  - Which of the problems we talked about would that solve?
  - What from the current support would you definitely keep?
- **Q 15** (Optional): Is there anything about your work or about loneliness that we have not covered and that you think is important?

## Closing (Closing, 3 min)

> That brings us to the end of the interview. Thank you very much for your time and for sharing your experience so openly – it is really valuable for the project.
>
> Here is what happens next: the recording will be transcribed and anonymised, and together with the other staff and service user interviews it will be analysed to identify good care moments and points where services could improve. These findings will feed into co-design workshops in which staff and service users agree priorities for improving support. Thank you again, and take care.

*Stop the recording after the spoken text. If the interview touched on distressing cases, check briefly how the interviewee is feeling and point to [Support resources] if needed.*
