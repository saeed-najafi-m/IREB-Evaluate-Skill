# Regulations and Standards: GOV.UK Notify, United Kingdom

No documents were provided, so this list is based only on official web sources, several of which could only be read in part (tables of contents or single provisions), and the NCSC (National Cyber Security Centre) and security.gov.uk pages could not be retrieved.

| ID | Designation | Type | Jurisdiction | Description | Relevance | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| REG 1 | United Kingdom General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), retained Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Law | National (United Kingdom) | The EU General Data Protection Regulation as kept in UK law after Brexit. It sets the core rules for processing personal data (any information about an identifiable person). It has been amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, for example on lawful processing, recognised legitimate interests, automated decision-making, international transfers and complaints. | Notify processes recipients' email addresses, mobile numbers, postal addresses and message content, so the system must support lawful processing, accuracy, data minimisation, retention limits (7 days by default, configurable after go-live) and security. | SRC 9, SRC 10, SRC 1, SRC 2 |
| REG 2 | Data Protection Act 2018 (2018 c. 12) | Law | National (United Kingdom) | UK Act that supplements the UK GDPR (Part 2 "General processing", with exemptions in Schedule 2) and sets the powers of the Information Commissioner; it has also been amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. | It adds UK-specific conditions and exemptions for public-sector processing of personal data, which the requirements on Notify's data handling and on each sending organisation's use of it must reflect. | SRC 8, SRC 10 |
| REG 3 | The Privacy and Electronic Communications (EC Directive) Regulations 2003 (S.I. 2003/2426), PECR | Regulation | National (United Kingdom) | Statutory instrument on electronic communications. Its definition of "electronic mail" expressly includes SMS (text messages), and regulation 22 bans unsolicited direct-marketing electronic mail without prior consent. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amended it, including rules on cookies, direct marketing by charities (in force 5 February 2026), breach notification time limits and enforcement powers. | Emails and text messages sent through Notify are "electronic mail", so the system must make sure messages are transactional or subscribed to, never unsolicited marketing. The cookie rules also apply to the Notify website. | SRC 7, SRC 10 |
| REG 4 | The Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018 (S.I. 2018/952) | Regulation | National (United Kingdom) | Requires public sector bodies to make their websites and mobile apps accessible, which it defines as "perceivable, operable, understandable and robust". It covers the disproportionate-burden assessment, the published accessibility statement, monitoring and enforcement, and lists exemptions such as third-party content the body does not fund, develop or control. | The Notify web interface used by government teams, and any web content linked from messages, are public sector websites and must meet these accessibility duties, including an accessibility statement. | SRC 3, SRC 4 |
| REG 5 | Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, level AA | Standard | International | Technical accessibility success criteria published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C). GOV.UK guidance names WCAG 2.2 AA as the level that public sector websites and apps must meet. | It gives the testable accessibility criteria for the Notify web interface and for HTML content such as email templates. | SRC 4 |
| REG 6 | Equality Act 2010 (2010 c. 15), including section 149 (public sector equality duty) | Law | National (England, Scotland and Wales) | Prohibits discrimination and requires reasonable adjustments for disabled people. Section 149 requires public authorities to have due regard to eliminating discrimination, advancing equality of opportunity and fostering good relations. In Northern Ireland the Disability Discrimination Act 1995 applies instead. | Communications sent through Notify, and the service itself, must not exclude people with protected characteristics, for example by offering alternative formats and channels such as letters as well as digital messages. | SRC 11, SRC 4 |
| REG 7 | Welsh Language (Wales) Measure 2011 (2011 nawm 1) | Law | Regional (Wales) | Measure of the National Assembly for Wales (now the Senedd) that gives Welsh official status. It lets Welsh language standards be imposed on bodies, sets up the Welsh Language Commissioner, and provides for enforcement and civil penalties. | Bodies subject to Welsh language standards that send emails, texts or letters through Notify may need bilingual (Welsh and English) templates and content. | SRC 13 |
| REG 8 | Government Security Classifications Policy (GSCP) | Guideline | Organizational (HM Government) | The UK government's administrative system for classifying and protecting information assets at OFFICIAL, SECRET and TOP SECRET. Classed as a guideline because it is an administrative policy rather than legislation, although it is mandatory within government. | Notify is designed for OFFICIAL information, including OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE, and must not be used for SECRET or TOP SECRET, which sets the security level the system must be specified for. | SRC 12, SRC 2 |
| REG 9 | NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF), applied through GovAssure | Guideline | Organizational (HM Government) | The NCSC's framework of cyber security outcomes. GovAssure is the government's cyber assurance approach for checking systems against it. Notify's security page names both as references, together with CHECK penetration testing (an NCSC scheme) and personnel screening (BPSS, the Baseline Personnel Security Standard, and SC, Security Check vetting). | They set the expected security controls and assurance evidence for Notify as a government system, such as risk management, protective measures, detection and incident response. | SRC 2 |
| REG 10 | Service Standard (GOV.UK Service Manual) | Guideline | National (United Kingdom public sector) | The 14-point standard for building and running government services, published by the Government Digital Service. Its points include "Make sure everyone can use the service", "Create a secure service which protects users' privacy" and "Operate a reliable service". | Notify has passed a live service assessment against this standard, so its points set baseline quality requirements for the system's design, accessibility, security and operation. | SRC 5, SRC 2 |
| REG 11 | GOV.UK Service Manual: Sending emails and text messages | Guideline | National (United Kingdom public sector) | Guidance on when and how to send government emails and text messages. It covers transactional messages versus subscription messages, which must be explicitly requested and offer an unsubscribe option, protection against spam and phishing (GOV.UK links only, no attachments or requests for personal details), and writing style. | Notify's terms of use require messages to meet Service Manual standards, so these rules shape template, content and unsubscribe requirements. | SRC 6, SRC 1 |
| REG 12 | GOV.UK Notify terms of use | Guideline | Organizational (GOV.UK Notify / Government Digital Service) | Terms that users accept when they create a Notify account. Classed as a guideline because they are contractual terms of the service operator, not legislation or a formal standard. They ban unsolicited messages, require consent and accurate data, and require multi-factor authentication on work-managed devices, encrypted API keys rotated at least every year, immediate breach reporting, and NCSC Cyber Essentials and protective DNS (PDNS) as recommended minimum measures. | They set organizational obligations on the sending services and on Notify itself, such as 30 days' notice of changes to the terms. Requirements for account management, API key handling and message content must reflect them. | SRC 1 |
