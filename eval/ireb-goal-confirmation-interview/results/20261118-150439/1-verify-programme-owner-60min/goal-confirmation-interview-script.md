# Interview Script: GOV.UK Verify

Goal confirmation interview with the GDS programme owner responsible for Verify's delivery and its adoption across government departments ([Name]), Government Digital Service, Cabinet Office, to agree on 2 main goals in 60 minutes.

Buffer: 6 min. If time runs short, skip first: Q 2, Q 8 optional follow-ups, Q 4 optional follow-ups, Q 5 optional follow-ups, Q 6 optional follow-ups, Q 9 optional follow-ups

## Objective

Verify, update, or dismiss the three goals taken from the Verify business cases, find missing goals, and agree with the programme owner on the 2 main goals of GOV.UK Verify.

## Completion Criteria

- For each of G 1 to G 3, the higher goal it serves is named, it is classified as a goal or a solution, and a decision is recorded: confirm, reword, merge, split, or dismiss.
- For each goal not dismissed, a measure (metric, target value, and date) is recorded.
- Missing goals have been asked for, and each new goal has a wording and a measure.
- Overlapping and conflicting goals have been asked for, and any that were named are recorded.
- All confirmed and new goals are ranked, 2 main goals are agreed, and the interviewer has read them back and the interviewee has confirmed them.

## Previously Collected Goals

Source: the business-case targets for service and user take-up, as reported in the provided document (2015 and 2016 business cases). This is the only goal list provided.

| ID | Goal |
|---|---|
| G 1 | Number of users who have signed up to use Verify: 25 million people use Verify by 2020 (forecast in both the 2015 and 2016 business cases). |
| G 2 | Number of government services that use Verify: 46 services connect to Verify by March 2018 (2016 business case). |
| G 3 | Verification success rate (the proportion of people who can sign up for a Verify user account having had their identities successfully confirmed by a provider): 90% (2015 business case). |

## Welcome and Purpose (Opening, 4 min)

> Thank you for taking the time today. I'm [Interviewer name], and I'm working on the requirements for GOV.UK Verify. In the next hour I'd like to go through the goals we found in the programme's business cases with you: check whether each one still holds, find any that are missing, and agree on the two main goals at the end. There are no right or wrong answers. I'm interested in your view as the person responsible for Verify's delivery and adoption.

*Note: Do not promise confidentiality or say how the results will be shared unless this has been agreed beforehand. Make clear that the targets are being discussed as targets. Do not suggest whether they were met.*

- **Q 1** (Required): Is it all right with you if I take notes during the interview?
- **Q 2** (Optional): How would you describe your responsibilities for Verify?

## Context and Goals Overview (Main, 5 min)

> According to the document we have, Verify's performance can be assessed against the expectations the programme's business cases set for service and user take-up. The document names three targets: 25 million people using Verify by 2020, from both the 2015 and 2016 business cases; 46 government services connected to Verify by March 2018, from the 2016 business case; and a verification success rate of 90%, from the 2015 business case. The verification success rate is the proportion of people who can sign up for a Verify user account having had their identities successfully confirmed by a provider. I'll show you these as G 1 to G 3.

*Note: Show the table of previously collected goals. Use only what is in the document. The document is a later report on the business cases, so it is not clear whether the targets are still current. Leave that question to the interviewee.*

- **Q 3** (Required; goals: G 1, G 2, G 3): How would you describe, in your own words, what each of these three targets means?
  - Optional: If the interviewee's understanding differs from the document: Where does this difference come from?

## Goal G 1: Users Signed Up (Main, 9 min)

*Note: Ask about the goal's purpose before its target value. "Use" and "signed up" may mean different things here: signed-up accounts versus people who actually use them. If the interviewee names a different number or date, record it as given.*

- **Q 4** (Required; goals: G 1): What should the number of people signed up to Verify achieve for the government?
  - Which higher goal does this goal serve?
  - Is this a goal in itself, or a means of reaching another goal?
  - Do you confirm, reword, merge, split, or dismiss this goal?
  - If not dismissed: How should this goal be measured, with which target value, and by when?
  - Optional: How do you see the current state of user take-up compared with this target?
  - Optional: What does "using Verify" mean to you, as opposed to having signed up?

## Goal G 2: Government Services Connected (Main, 8 min)

*Note: Possible overlap with G 1: user numbers may depend on how many services connect. Raise this in the overlaps question, not here.*

- **Q 5** (Required; goals: G 2): What should the number of government services connected to Verify achieve?
  - Which higher goal does this goal serve?
  - Is this a goal in itself, or a means of reaching another goal?
  - Do you confirm, reword, merge, split, or dismiss this goal?
  - If not dismissed: How should this goal be measured, with which target value, and by when?
  - Optional: How do you see the current state of service take-up compared with this target?
  - Optional: What does a service count as "connected" to Verify?

## Goal G 3: Verification Success Rate (Main, 8 min)

*Note: Possible conflict: a higher success rate and stricter identity assurance may pull in different directions, and so may G 3 and G 1. Keep this for Q 8. The definition depends on the identity providers ("a provider"), so ask about responsibility without naming organizations.*

- **Q 6** (Required; goals: G 3): What should the verification success rate achieve for users and for government?
  - Which higher goal does this goal serve?
  - Is this a goal in itself, or a means of reaching another goal?
  - Do you confirm, reword, merge, split, or dismiss this goal?
  - If not dismissed: How should this goal be measured, with which target value, and by when?
  - Optional: How do you see the current verification success rate compared with this target?
  - Optional: Who is responsible for reaching this rate?

## Missing Goals (Main, 9 min)

- **Q 7** (Required): Which goals for Verify are not covered by the three we have discussed?
  - If a new goal is named: How would you word this goal?
  - If a new goal is named: How should this goal be measured, with which target value, and by when?
  - Optional: Which goals would other stakeholders, such as government departments, users, or identity providers, add?
  - Optional: Which goals do you have for cost?
  - Optional: Which goals do you have for user experience and inclusion?
  - Optional: Which goals do you have for quality, such as security, privacy, and availability?
  - Optional: Which goals do you have for compliance?
  - Optional: Which goals do you have for running Verify after launch?

## Overlaps and Conflicts (Main, 4 min)

*Note: Possible links to listen for, but do not suggest them: G 1 and G 2 (take-up of services drives user numbers), G 1 and G 3 (more sign-ups versus a higher success rate), and any new goal on security or privacy against G 3.*

- **Q 8** (Required; goals: G 1, G 2, G 3): Where do the goals we have discussed overlap or conflict?
  - Optional: If a conflict is named: How should this conflict be resolved?

## Prioritization (Closing, 5 min)

*Note: List all confirmed, reworded, split, merged, and new goals visibly before asking. Read the two main goals back word for word.*

- **Q 9** (Required; goals: G 1, G 2, G 3): How would you rank all the goals we have now, from most to least important?
  - Optional: What is the reason for this ranking?
- **Q 10** (Required; goals: G 1, G 2, G 3): Do you agree that the two highest-ranked goals are the main goals of Verify?
  - If not: Which two goals should be the main goals instead?
  - Let me read the two main goals back to you: [Main goal 1], [Main goal 2]. Is this correct?

## Thanks and Next Steps (Closing, 2 min)

> Thank you very much for your time and your input. I'll write up the results, including the decisions on each goal, the new goals, and the two main goals we agreed on, and send them to you for review by [date]. If anything else comes to mind in the meantime, please contact me at [contact details].

## Assumptions and Open Points

- The domain (government online identity verification), the organization, the number of main goals (2), and the available time (60 minutes) were taken from the brief. The interview language is English, the language of the request.
- The goal list is the set of business-case targets in the provided document. The document reports them later ("as later reported"), so they may not be the current goals. Whether they were met and whether they are still appropriate are left to the interviewee (Q 4 to Q 6). The interviewer note in the opening says not to suggest either.
- Each target was recorded as one goal, keeping the metric name, value, date, and source business case together. Example: G 1 combines "number of users signed up" with "25 million people would use Verify by 2020".
- The target dates (March 2018, 2020) are in the past. The date of the interview is unknown, so each goal's measurement follow-up asks for a target date again.
- No facts from outside the document were used. This includes Verify's actual performance and its later history.
- Time allocation: 4 min opening, 5 min context, 9 min for G 1 (it has two extra optional follow-ups and may raise the "signed up" versus "use" question), 8 min each for G 2 and G 3, 9 min for missing goals, 4 min for overlaps and conflicts, 5 min for prioritization, 2 min for the end. That makes 54 min plus a 6 min buffer (10% of 60 min). No break is needed because the time is under 90 minutes.
- Decided without clear guidance: the prioritization questions (Q 9, Q 10) sit in the closing phase as the skill instructs. A separate closing section without questions follows them.
- Decided without clear guidance: Q 1 (permission to take notes) is a closed organizational question. Q 10 is a closed decision question. All other questions are open.
- Names of the interviewee and interviewer, the review date, and contact details are placeholders.
